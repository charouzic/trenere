# Injury Prevention

## Purpose

Keep niggles from becoming training interruptions.

## Core Rules

- Pain 0-2/10 is usually acceptable if stable and there is no gait change.
- Pain 3/10 requires caution and reduced load.
- Pain above 3/10 requires stopping or modifying.
- Any gait change means stop running.
- Worse next morning means the previous load was too high.
- Previous injury is a major future-injury risk factor.

## When To Use

- Onboarding injury history.
- Planning after pain reports.
- Return-to-running decisions.

## When Not To Use

- To diagnose injuries.
- To continue training through sharp, worsening, neurological, chest, or
  systemic symptoms.

## Common Mistakes

- Keeping workouts while reducing only easy volume.
- Returning to speed before easy volume is stable.
- Ignoring next-morning response.

## Coach Decision Rules

- Reintroduce volume before intensity.
- Remove speed first if pain appears during fast running.
- Shorten duration first if pain appears late in runs.
- Avoid hard workouts during return from niggle.

## Evidence Notes

- Previous injury predicts future injury: Strong evidence.
- Footwear as injury prevention: Limited evidence.
- Stretching as injury prevention: Limited evidence.
