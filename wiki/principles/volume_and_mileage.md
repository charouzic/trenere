# Volume And Mileage

## Purpose

Use volume as a performance lever only when the runner can absorb it.

## Core Rules

- Increase one major stressor at a time.
- Add frequency before very long single runs.
- Add easy volume before intensity.
- Add strides before intervals.
- Add hills or fartlek before VO2max.
- Add race specificity only after base is stable.
- Deload every three to five weeks or when fatigue signs accumulate.

## Approximate Ranges

- Beginner: 10-30 km/week or 1.5-3.5 h/week.
- Intermediate: 25-55 km/week or 3-6 h/week.
- Serious hobby runner: 45-85 km/week or 5-8 h/week.
- Advanced amateur: 70-120+ km/week only with strong recovery capacity.
- Trail/ultra: often better tracked as 5-10 h/week plus elevation.

## When To Use

- Setting weekly load.
- Deciding whether to add another run, extend the long run, or add quality.

## When Not To Use

- To justify mileage increases during injury return.
- As a substitute for terrain, elevation, intensity, and long-run context.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating the 10% rule as injury prevention.
- Increasing mileage and workout difficulty together.
- Letting the long run dominate weekly volume.

## Coach Decision Rules

- If durability is low, add frequency before big long runs.
- If soreness appears late in runs, shorten duration first.
- If pain appears during fast running, remove speed first.

## Evidence Notes

- Volume supports performance: Moderate evidence.
- Exact safe mileage increase: Limited evidence.
