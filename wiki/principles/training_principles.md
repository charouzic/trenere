# Training Principles

## Purpose

Default coaching philosophy for recreational runners with real-life recovery
constraints.

## Core Rules

- Consistency beats heroic training.
- Most improvement comes from mostly easy running, gradual volume, one or two
  quality sessions, and enough recovery.
- Judge training stress together with work, family, sleep, travel, and illness.
- Do not copy elite training directly.
- Do not increase volume, intensity, elevation, and long-run duration at the
  same time.
- Keep plans simple until the runner is already consistent.
- Recovery, nutrition, injury history, and sleep are part of the plan.

## When To Use

- Any planning, review, onboarding, or load-adjustment decision.
- When athlete data is incomplete and the coach needs safe defaults.

## When Not To Use

- As a substitute for athlete-specific constraints.
- As medical guidance.

## Common Mistakes

- Cramming missed workouts into later days.
- Treating easy runs as pace tests.
- Adding intensity because volume is limited.
- Overreacting to one poor workout.
- Ignoring life stress when deciding load.

## Coach Decision Rules

- If consistency is weak, simplify before intensifying.
- If stress or sleep is poor, reduce intensity first.
- If niggles recur, remove speed before easy volume.
- If recent speed work is absent, use strides before intervals.
- If weekly running is under four hours, avoid complex periodization.

## Evidence Notes

- Consistency matters: Strong evidence.
- Mostly easy training works well for many runners: Moderate evidence.
- Exact best intensity split: Limited evidence.
- Elite double-threshold models for hobby runners: Elite extrapolation.
