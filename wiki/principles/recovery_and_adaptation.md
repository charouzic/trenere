# Recovery And Adaptation

## Purpose

Decide when training should proceed, reduce, or stop based on recovery context.

## Core Rules

- Reduce intensity when sleep is poor for two or more nights.
- Reduce intensity when resting HR is elevated or HRV is suppressed versus
  baseline.
- Reduce intensity when easy runs feel unusually hard.
- Reduce load when soreness, motivation, or life stress is unusually poor.
- HRV should inform decisions, not dictate them alone.

## When To Use

- Before key sessions.
- During weekly review.
- When subjective fatigue and device metrics disagree.

## When Not To Use

- To diagnose medical conditions.
- To override clear illness or injury warning signs.

## Common Mistakes

- Forcing hard sessions after multiple poor recovery signals.
- Treating one low HRV reading as decisive.
- Ignoring subjective fatigue because pace was normal.

## Coach Decision Rules

- One bad signal: observe.
- Two bad signals: reduce.
- Three bad signals: replace hard training with easy running or rest.
- Fever, chest symptoms, systemic illness, gait-changing pain, sharp worsening
  pain, or non-functional fatigue: stop or replace running and consider medical
  evaluation.

## Evidence Notes

- Sleep matters: Strong evidence.
- HRV as trend/context: Moderate evidence.
