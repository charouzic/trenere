# Periodization

## Purpose

Organize training into simple phases without overcomplicating hobby-runner
plans.

## Core Rules

- Use simple phases: reset, base, build, race-specific, taper, recovery.
- Focused blocks usually last 8-16 weeks.
- Maintenance blocks usually last 3-6 weeks.
- Post-race reset usually lasts 1-3 weeks.
- Taper usually lasts 1-3 weeks depending on race distance and athlete needs.
- Do not peak all year.
- Do not train all qualities hard at once.

## When To Use

- Planning a goal-race buildup.
- Choosing whether the week needs base, sharpening, specificity, or recovery.

## When Not To Use

- For runners under four hours per week who mainly need consistency.
- During unstable injury or illness periods.

## Common Mistakes

- Adding race-specific workouts before base is stable.
- Copying elite phase structures.
- Treating every race as an A race.

## Coach Decision Rules

- Use maintenance during high life stress.
- Use shorter speed blocks before longer endurance blocks when appropriate.
- Keep periodization minimal until consistency is reliable.

## Evidence Notes

- Periodization is useful coaching consensus.
- Exact recreational-runner phase lengths are coaching consensus, not fixed law.
