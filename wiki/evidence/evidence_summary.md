# Evidence Summary

## Purpose

Keep coaching claims honest and conservative.

## Evidence Labels

- Strong evidence: robust enough to guide defaults confidently.
- Moderate evidence: useful but still context-dependent.
- Limited evidence: plausible but uncertain or athlete-dependent.
- Coaching consensus: widely used practice with practical support.
- Elite extrapolation: may not transfer to recreational runners.
- Anecdotal / uncertain: use cautiously and label clearly.

## Strong Claims

- Sleep matters.
- Previous injury predicts future injury.
- Low energy availability is harmful.
- Consistency matters.

## Moderate Claims

- Volume supports performance when absorbed.
- Mostly easy intensity distribution works well.
- HIIT improves fitness but is costly.
- Strength can improve running economy.
- HRV can help when used as trend and context.

## Limited Or Uncertain Claims

- Exact best intensity split.
- Exact safe mileage increase.
- Stretching as injury prevention.
- Footwear as injury prevention.
- Fasted training for recreational runners.
- Mandatory back-to-back long runs.
- Elite double-threshold models for hobby runners.

## Coach Decision Rules

- Label uncertain claims.
- Prefer conservative defaults for recreational runners.
- Do not present elite extrapolation as a rule.
